# Historical Workbook Data Contract

This contract governs the four supplied league workbooks and every dataset generated by
`scripts/history_importer.py`. Its purpose is to make the historical matches useful for
training—especially the half-time/full-time (HT/FT) target—without leaking post-match or
unverified late-market information into pre-match features.

## Supported source

The importer accepts one workbook for each supported worksheet name:

| Worksheet | League key | Output stem |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `巴甲` | `brazil_serie_a` | `brazil-serie-a` |
| `挪超` | `norway_eliteserien` | `norway-eliteserien` |
| `日职` | `japan_j1` | `japan-j1` |
| `美职联` | `usa_mls` | `usa-mls` |

The supplied bundle contains 9,211 finished matches across only these four competitions:
2,479 Brazil Serie A, 2,238 Japan J1, 1,554 Norway Eliteserien, and 2,940 MLS. It contains no
Korean K League sample and provides no validated transfer claim for Korean predictions.

Each workbook must contain exactly one worksheet, 87 columns, two exact header rows, and at
least one finished match. The first row defines merged market groups and the second row
defines individual fields. Both rows are part of the schema; renaming, shifting, or adding a
column fails the import.

The importer also fails closed on:

- any Excel formula, even when the cell contains a cached value;
- non-continuous source identifiers or a blank row inside the data region;
- a league value that differs from the worksheet name, or a status other than `完`;
- missing/equal team names, malformed or implausible scores, and total-goal conflicts;
- source `半全场` or `胜平负` labels that do not agree with the score;
- duplicate fixtures; and
- kickoff time moving backwards within a season, an invalid calendar rollover, or an
  unsupported/implicit timezone.

Strings are trimmed and normalized to Unicode NFC. This does not silently merge aliases or
translated team names; a future alias table must be explicit, versioned, and tested.

## Kickoff and season rules

`比赛时间` stores only `MM-DD HH:MM`, while `年份` is the competition season rather than a
guaranteed calendar year. The operator must therefore supply the source timezone explicitly.
The default, `Asia/Shanghai`, matches the provenance assumption for the supplied export; do
not reuse it for another source without verification.

Within each season, the importer starts in the season year. One transition from an October,
November, or December row to a January, February, or March row advances the calendar year by
one. This is required for Brazil's 2020 season, whose January 2021 matches still carry season
`2020`. Every inferred transition is written to `manifest.json`; more than one transition or
any resulting backwards timestamp is rejected.

The normalized time fields mean:

- `source_kickoff`: timezone-aware local kickoff reconstructed from the workbook;
- `source_timezone`: the explicit operator-supplied IANA zone;
- `kickoff_utc`: the same instant in UTC; and
- `date`: the UTC calendar date used by the existing score-model interface.

`season` always preserves the source season label. Never reconstruct chronological order by
concatenating `season` and `比赛时间` outside this importer.

## Generated datasets

Run the importer into local ignored runtime storage rather than committing the supplied raw
workbooks or row-level exports:

```text
python scripts/history_importer.py SOURCE_OR_DIRECTORY \
  --output-dir .codex/soccer-predict/datasets \
  --source-timezone Asia/Shanghai
```

For each league it writes two CSV files and one bundle manifest.

### `*-scores.csv`

The score file contains:

```text
date,home_team,away_team,home_goals,away_goals,
half_home_goals,half_away_goals,half_result,full_result,htft_result,
league_key,league,season,competition_regime,round,source_row,
source_kickoff,source_timezone,kickoff_utc
```

Only `date`, `home_team`, and `away_team` are pre-match input fields in the minimum model
contract. Goals and result codes are supervised outcome labels. They must never be supplied
as features for the same fixture.

The normalized result codes are `H` (home win), `D` (draw), and `A` (away win):

- `half_result` is derived from the half-time score;
- `full_result` is derived from the full-time score; and
- `htft_result` concatenates them into one of the nine targets `HH`, `HD`, `HA`, `DH`,
  `DD`, `DA`, `AH`, `AD`, or `AA`.

For example, 0-0 at half-time and 2-1 at full-time becomes `half_result=D`,
`full_result=H`, and `htft_result=DH`. The source text label is used only as an integrity
cross-check; the normalized target is always re-derived from verified scores.

These labels make the data suitable for chronological HT/FT training and calibration. They
do not by themselves authorize an actionable HT/FT recommendation: promotion still requires
future clean live-forward validation, calibration across all nine classes, adequate coverage,
and pre-kickoff market evidence under the active policy.

`competition_regime` is a dataset-context boundary, not a team-strength feature. Regular
seasons use `regular`. All 180 supplied Japan J1 2026 fixtures fall inside the regional
special-format batch and use `2026_vision_regional`; the importer applies that label to both
score and opening-market rows. The J.League's official special-season overview describes the
two ten-club regional groups and 180-match round, while its regular schedule lists 7 August
2026 as Matchweek 1 of the 2026/27 J1 season:
[`special format`](https://www.jleague.jp/special/2026specialseason/j1/),
[`regular schedule`](https://www.jleague.jp/en/j1/match/).

### `*-opening-markets.csv`

The market file contains one row per match and bookmaker, limited to the source columns marked
as opening full-time 1X2, Asian handicap, and total-goals prices. Completeness flags are
included because missingness is systematic for some bookmakers and years. Do not mean-impute
those gaps.

The workbooks do not record the exact snapshot collection time. Consequently these prices are
`research_only_untimestamped_opening_snapshot`: they may serve as a benchmark or a separately
reported experiment, but they are not automatically joined into the production score model.
The files contain no first-half 1X2 odds and no nine-way HT/FT odds, so they cannot establish
HT/FT value or expected value on their own.

All closing 1X2, Asian, and total prices are excluded from every generated CSV. They are
validated only structurally and listed as quarantined in the manifest. Rankings, source
total/result labels, and other post-match-derived fields are likewise excluded as training
features.

## Manifest and reproducibility

`manifest.json` records:

- importer and schema versions;
- source filename and SHA-256 hash;
- output filename, row count, and SHA-256 hash;
- league and season counts, UTC date range, and every calendar rollover;
- per-bookmaker opening-market completeness;
- the feature whitelist, outcome-label fields, research-only fields, quarantined fields,
  caveats, and deterministic bundle hash.

Regenerate the bundle after any source change instead of editing a CSV by hand. A different
source hash, timezone, schema, or importer version is a different dataset lineage and must
produce new validation evidence.

## Training and evaluation boundary

Train separate league models; do not pool disconnected competitions as if team strength were
on one scale. The registered production manager additionally uses
`regular-only-production-v1`: only `competition_regime=regular` enters production fitting or
formal fixed-season scoring, while special regimes retain only exclusion counts for drift auditing. Use expanding-window or rolling
chronological evaluation, keep complete kickoff groups together, and reserve a genuinely
unseen final period for a one-time promotion decision. Newly promoted or unseen teams require
an explicit fallback and separate reporting; the manager fails closed by default.

For HT/FT, report at least nine-class log loss, multiclass Brier score, per-class support and
recall, calibration, and coverage/abstention. Because rare classes can make raw hit rate look
better or worse by chance, a higher historical win rate alone is not sufficient evidence.
Compare against simple league-frequency and independent-half baselines, then retain a change
only when it improves a genuinely unseen forward sample without a material calibration or
data-quality regression. The current 2025 and partial-2026 cohorts were inspected during
final selector development, so they remain component/development evidence rather than an
untouched end-to-end test of `probability_top2_v3_post_selection`.

The fixed-season evaluator binds each artifact to the importer manifest, score-file hashes,
opening-market hashes when enabled, and fixture identities. Non-promoted fit or bootstrap
parameters require `--experimental-override`; those runs receive configuration-experiment
split roles and cannot be presented as promotion evidence. When loading a saved artifact,
validate it against the exact dataset directory or manifest so source binding is checked, not
only the outer JSON hash.

Historical rows are valid training evidence, not strict out-of-sample betting records. Keep
the old prediction/review cohort for audit and learning, but never blend it with forward
predictions when claiming live win rate or ROI.
